## How much water each person needs
Store at least 1 gallon of water per person per day, for drinking and basic hygiene. Florida emergency managers recommend at least 7 days of water and food per person for hurricane season. Children, nursing mothers, sick people, and anyone in very hot weather may need more; in extreme heat, water needs can double. Do not replace water with soda, coffee, or alcohol, which can dehydrate you. Remember extra water for pets.

## Which water needs treating
Treat any water you are not sure about before drinking it, cooking with it, brushing teeth, or making ice or baby formula. This includes tap water during a boil-water notice and any cloudy tap water. Do not use water from flooded areas. Sealed, store-bought bottled water is the safest choice.
Source: Ready.gov - Water <https://www.ready.gov/water>

## Boiling water to make it safe
If water is cloudy, first let it settle, then pour it through a clean cloth, paper towel, or coffee filter. Bring the water to a full rolling boil for at least 1 minute (3 minutes above 5,000 feet elevation). Let it cool, then store it in clean, covered containers. Boiling is the best way to kill germs when you have heat.
Source: EPA - Emergency disinfection of drinking water <https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/emergency-disinfection-drinking-water>

## Using bleach to disinfect water
If you cannot boil water, use plain unscented household bleach that says 6% or 8.25% sodium hypochlorite. Do not use scented, color-safe, or bleach with added cleaners. Filter cloudy water first. Add 8 drops of 6% bleach, or 6 drops of 8.25% bleach, per gallon of water (2 drops per quart for either). Double the amount if the water is cloudy, colored, or very cold. Stir and wait 30 minutes. The water should smell slightly of chlorine; if not, add the same dose again and wait 15 more minutes.
Source: EPA - Emergency disinfection of drinking water <https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/emergency-disinfection-drinking-water>

## Storing water safely
Keep store-bought water sealed in its original bottles in a cool, dark place. If you fill your own containers, use food-grade containers. Clean them with dish soap, then sanitize with 1 teaspoon of unscented bleach in a quart of water. Replace water you stored yourself every 6 months.
Source: Ready.gov - Water <https://www.ready.gov/water>
