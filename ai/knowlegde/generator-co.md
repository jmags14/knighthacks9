## What the StormBox air alarm means
The StormBox air sensor detects that something unusual is in the air, such as fumes or smoke. It is not a certified carbon monoxide (CO) detector and cannot measure CO. If the air alarm goes off, treat it as a warning: get everyone into fresh air, check for a running generator, grill, or engine nearby, and use a real battery-powered CO alarm if you have one. If a CO alarm sounds, leave the building right away and call 911 if you can.
Source: StormBox team; CDC - After a hurricane <https://www.cdc.gov/hurricanes/safety/how-to-safely-stay-safe-after-a-hurricane-or-other-tropical-storm.html>

## Signs of carbon monoxide poisoning
Carbon monoxide has no color or smell. Early symptoms are often mistaken for the flu: headache, dizziness, weakness, upset stomach, vomiting, chest pain, and confusion. A large exposure can make someone pass out or die. People who are asleep or drunk can die before they notice any symptoms. Anyone can be poisoned, but babies, older adults, and people with heart disease, anemia, or breathing problems are affected more easily. If several people feel sick at once indoors, suspect CO and get outside.
Source: CDC - Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Basics <https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/about/index.html>

## Safe generator use
Never run a generator inside your home, garage, basement, or shed, even with doors and windows open. Run it outside, at least 20 feet away from windows, doors, and vents. Use a battery-powered or battery-backup CO alarm whenever a generator or other fuel-burning equipment is running. You may not be able to power everything at once, so decide in advance what matters most, such as medical devices and the refrigerator.
Source: CDC - Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Basics <https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/about/index.html>; Ready.gov - Power Outages <https://www.ready.gov/power-outages>

## Grills, camp stoves and heating
Never burn charcoal indoors, and never use a portable gas camp stove inside. Never use a gas oven, stove, grill, or clothes dryer to heat your home. Never run a car or truck inside a garage, even with the garage door open. If you use a vehicle to charge phones or stay cool, park it in an open, well-ventilated area outside.
Source: CDC - Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Basics <https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/about/index.html>; Ready.gov - Power Outages <https://www.ready.gov/power-outages>

## How common CO poisoning is
In the United States, more than 400 people die each year from accidental carbon monoxide poisoning not linked to fires, more than 100,000 go to an emergency department, and more than 14,000 are hospitalized. Generator use after storms is a well-known cause. Put CO alarms near every sleeping area and replace them as the maker says or about every 5 years.
Source: CDC - Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Basics <https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/about/index.html>
