## Where to shelter inside during a hurricane
If you were not ordered to evacuate, go to a small interior room, closet, or hallway on the lowest floor that is not at risk of flooding. Put as many walls as possible between you and the outside. Stay away from windows, skylights, and glass doors, even if they are covered. Close all inside doors and brace outside doors. In a two-story house, use an interior room on the first floor. In a multi-story building away from water, go to the first or second floor and stay in interior halls. If winds become extreme, lie on the floor under a sturdy table.
Source: National Weather Service - Hurricane Safety <https://www.weather.gov/media/safety/hurricane_safety.pdf>; NWS - Hurricane Action <https://www.weather.gov/safety/hurricane-action>

## The eye of the hurricane: the calm is temporary
When the eye passes over, the wind can suddenly drop and the sky may even clear. This can look like the storm is over, but it is not. After the eye passes, the wind returns quickly at hurricane force from the opposite direction. Stay inside during the calm. Do not go outside to check damage or move cars. On StormBox, a "possible eye" message means pressure stopped falling near its lowest point; it is a reason to stay put, not to go out.
Source: National Weather Service - Hurricane Safety <https://www.weather.gov/media/safety/hurricane_safety.pdf>; NWS - Hurricane Action <https://www.weather.gov/safety/hurricane-action>

## Storm surge and flooding are the biggest killers
Flooding from storm surge, along with wind, is a major cause of hurricane deaths. People on the coast, on barrier islands, or near rivers and flood plains should evacuate when told. Tropical storms have weaker winds than hurricanes but can still bring life-threatening inland flooding. Moving water only 6 inches deep can sweep you off your feet, and standing water may be electrified by power lines.
Source: National Weather Service - Hurricane Safety <https://www.weather.gov/media/safety/hurricane_safety.pdf>

## Tornadoes during a hurricane
Hurricanes often spawn tornadoes, sometimes with little warning. If a tornado warning is issued, go to a basement, safe room, or interior room away from windows, and bring pets if there is time. Mobile homes are not safe in a tornado; move to a sturdy building if you can do so before the storm arrives.
Source: National Weather Service - Hurricane Safety <https://www.weather.gov/media/safety/hurricane_safety.pdf>; NWS - Tornado safety <https://www.weather.gov/safety/tornado-during>

## Stay inside until officials say it is safe
Stay indoors during the storm and until local officials confirm it is safe to go out. Hazards such as flooding, downed power lines, and debris can remain even when the weather seems calm. Hurricane season in Florida runs from June 1 to November 30, with the most activity from mid-August to mid-October.
