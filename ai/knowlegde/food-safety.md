## How long the fridge and freezer keep food safe
Keep the refrigerator and freezer doors closed as much as possible. A closed refrigerator keeps food safe for up to 4 hours without power. A full freezer holds a safe temperature for about 48 hours, and a half-full freezer for about 24 hours, if the door stays closed. A thermometer in the fridge and freezer tells you the real temperature. Many foods must be thrown away if they were at 40 degrees F (4 C) or warmer for more than 2 hours.
Source: FoodSafety.gov <https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/food-safety-during-power-outage>

## Refrigerator foods to throw away
After the power has been out for more than 4 hours, or if food was above 40 degrees F for more than 2 hours, throw away: raw or cooked meat, poultry, fish, and seafood; lunch meat, hot dogs, bacon, and sausage; milk, cream, yogurt, sour cream, and eggs; soft and shredded cheeses; leftovers, soups, casseroles, and gravy; cooked pasta, rice, and potatoes; cut fruit and cut vegetables; meat, egg, or tuna salads; custards, puddings, and cream-filled pastries; opened creamy dressings and opened spaghetti sauce.
Source: FoodSafety.gov <https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/food-safety-during-power-outage>

## Refrigerator foods that are usually still safe
These are generally safe to keep even after the fridge warms up: hard cheeses like cheddar, Swiss, and parmesan; butter and margarine; whole uncut fruit and vegetables; opened fruit juice and canned fruit; bread, tortillas, muffins, and fruit pies; peanut butter, jelly, mustard, ketchup, pickles, and soy sauce; opened vinegar-based dressings. Throw away anything with an odd smell, color, or texture.
Source: FoodSafety.gov <https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/food-safety-during-power-outage>

## Can I refreeze thawed food?
Food can be refrozen if it still has ice crystals or is at 40 degrees F or colder, though the quality may be lower. Meat, poultry, and seafood can be refrozen only if they still have ice crystals and feel cold. Throw away ice cream and frozen yogurt that partly thawed, and thawed cakes with custard or cheese fillings. Hard cheese, plain bread, flour, and nuts can be refrozen.
Source: FoodSafety.gov <https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/food-safety-during-power-outage>

## When in doubt, throw it out
Never taste food to decide if it is safe. Spoiled food can make you sick without smelling or looking bad. Throw away any food that touched flood water. Getting sick from food during an outage is especially dangerous when clean water and medical care are hard to reach.
Source: FoodSafety.gov <https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/food-safety-during-power-outage>
