## Basic emergency kit
A basic kit includes: water (1 gallon per person per day), non-perishable food, a battery-powered or hand-crank radio (ideally a NOAA Weather Radio with alerts), a flashlight, a first aid kit, extra batteries, a whistle to signal for help, dust masks, plastic sheeting and duct tape, wet wipes, garbage bags and ties for sanitation, a wrench or pliers to turn off utilities, a manual can opener, local paper maps, and a phone with chargers and a backup battery. Florida emergency managers recommend at least 7 days of food and water per person.

## Extra items to add
Also consider: prescription and over-the-counter medicines, glasses and contact lens supplies, baby formula, bottles, and diapers, pet food and extra water, cash, copies of important documents such as IDs and insurance in a waterproof container, a change of clothes and sturdy shoes, a blanket or sleeping bag per person, a fire extinguisher, matches in a waterproof container, soap and hand sanitizer, hygiene items, paper plates and utensils, paper and pencil, and games or books for children.
Source: Ready.gov - Build a Kit <https://www.ready.gov/kit>

## Keeping the kit ready
Store canned food in a cool, dry place and boxed food in sealed plastic or metal containers. Replace anything that has expired. Review the kit every year, and update it when your family's needs change, such as a new baby, a new pet, or a new medicine. Keep your car's gas tank at least half full during hurricane season.
