## Should we evacuate or stay?
If local officials order an evacuation for your area, leave right away. Know your evacuation zone before a storm. People living on the coast, on barrier islands, near rivers or flood plains, or in mobile homes are most at risk from storm surge, flooding, and wind. If you are not under an evacuation order and your home is sturdy and not in a flood zone, you may be able to shelter in place.
Source: Florida Division of Emergency Management <https://www.floridadisaster.org/news-media/news/20260504-florida-division-of-emergency-management-calls-on-floridians-to-prepare-ahead-of-hurricane-season-with-2026-florida-hurricane-preparedness-week/>; NWS - Hurricane Safety <https://www.weather.gov/media/safety/hurricane_safety.pdf>

## Go tens of miles, not hundreds
Most Floridians only need to travel tens of miles inland, out of the surge zone, to reach a safer place, such as a friend's home or a shelter. Long trips can leave you stuck on crowded roads when the storm arrives. Plan your route, where you will stay, and how your household will stay in touch, including children, older adults, and pets.
Source: Florida Division of Emergency Management <https://www.floridadisaster.org/news-media/news/20260504-florida-division-of-emergency-management-calls-on-floridians-to-prepare-ahead-of-hurricane-season-with-2026-florida-hurricane-preparedness-week/>

## On the road during an evacuation
Keep your gas tank at least half full during hurricane season (keep an electric car between 50% and 80% charged, or as the maker advises). Never drive around barricades; responders use them to keep traffic out of danger. Never drive into flood water: about 12 inches of moving water can carry away most cars. Avoid bridges over fast-moving water.
Source: Florida Division of Emergency Management <https://www.floridadisaster.org/news-media/news/20260504-florida-division-of-emergency-management-calls-on-floridians-to-prepare-ahead-of-hurricane-season-with-2026-florida-hurricane-preparedness-week/>; Ready.gov - Floods <https://www.ready.gov/floods>

## People with special needs
People who need help evacuating, or who depend on power for medical equipment, can sign up ahead of time with Florida's Special Needs Registry (FloridaDisaster.org/SNR). Bring medicines, medical supplies, and copies of prescriptions when you leave.
Source: Florida Division of Emergency Management <https://www.floridadisaster.org/news-media/news/20260504-florida-division-of-emergency-management-calls-on-floridians-to-prepare-ahead-of-hurricane-season-with-2026-florida-hurricane-preparedness-week/>

## Returning home after an evacuation
Wait until local officials say it is safe to return. Avoid driving unless it is necessary, since roads may be flooded, blocked, or damaged. Do not enter a damaged building until officials say it is safe.
Source: Ready.gov - Floods <https://www.ready.gov/floods>
