## Calling 911
StormBox cannot call 911 or send messages for you. If someone's life is in danger and your phone has service, call 911. If a call fails, wait about 10 seconds before trying again so the network is not overloaded. Voice calls to 911 are best. Text-to-911 works in some areas but not everywhere; if texting is not available, you should get an automatic reply telling you to call instead. The rule is: call if you can, text if you can't.
Source: FCC - Text-to-911 <https://www.fcc.gov/what-you-need-know-about-text-911>; FCC and FEMA tips <https://dhhr.wv.gov/healthprep/plan/howtoprepare/familyprep/Documents/FCC%20Tips_for_Communicating_2011.pdf>

## Text instead of calling for non-emergencies
During and after a disaster, phone networks get overloaded. For anything that is not an emergency, send a text, email, or social media message instead of making a voice call; text messages often get through when calls do not. Keep any calls short and share only the important information.
Source: FCC and FEMA tips <https://dhhr.wv.gov/healthprep/plan/howtoprepare/familyprep/Documents/FCC%20Tips_for_Communicating_2011.pdf>

## Making your phone battery last
Lower the screen brightness, turn on airplane mode when you don't need the phone, and close apps you are not using. Use low power mode if your phone has it. You can charge a phone in a car, but only if the car is parked outside in an open area, never in a garage, and only once it is safe to go outside.
Source: FCC and FEMA tips <https://dhhr.wv.gov/healthprep/plan/howtoprepare/familyprep/Documents/FCC%20Tips_for_Communicating_2011.pdf>

## Family contacts
Save "In Case of Emergency" (ICE) contacts in your phone and tell those people they are listed. Pick an out-of-town contact for everyone to check in with; long-distance calls or texts can be easier to get through than local ones. Keep a paper list of important phone numbers in case your phone dies. A traditional corded landline phone may still work when the power is out, but internet-based home phones will not.
Source: FCC and FEMA tips <https://dhhr.wv.gov/healthprep/plan/howtoprepare/familyprep/Documents/FCC%20Tips_for_Communicating_2011.pdf>

## Feeling overwhelmed
Disasters are stressful, and it is normal to feel scared, anxious, or exhausted. The Disaster Distress Helpline is free and open all the time: call or text 1-800-985-5990 (press 2 for Spanish). People with hearing loss can use their usual relay service to call the same number.
Source: CDC - After a hurricane <https://www.cdc.gov/hurricanes/safety/how-to-safely-stay-safe-after-a-hurricane-or-other-tropical-storm.html>
