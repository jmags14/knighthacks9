## Downed power lines
Always treat a fallen power line as live. Stay far away from it and from anything it touches, such as fences, cars, puddles, or trees. The ground can be energized up to about 35 feet around a downed line. Never step into water near a downed line. If someone is touching a line, do not touch them; call 911. Report downed lines to your electric company. Also watch for lines hanging overhead.
Source: ESFI - Downed power lines <https://www.kcc.ks.gov/images/PDFs/electric/ESFI-Downed-Power-Lines.pdf>; CDC - After a hurricane <https://www.cdc.gov/hurricanes/safety/how-to-safely-stay-safe-after-a-hurricane-or-other-tropical-storm.html>

## If a power line falls on your car
Stay inside the car. Honk the horn to call for help and warn others to stay back. Only leave if the car catches fire: jump out with both feet together, without touching the car and the ground at the same time, then shuffle away in small steps keeping your feet together and on the ground.
Source: ESFI - Downed power lines <https://www.kcc.ks.gov/images/PDFs/electric/ESFI-Downed-Power-Lines.pdf>

## Damaged homes and buildings
Do not go into a damaged building until local officials say it is safe. Leave right away if you hear shifting or unusual noises, which can mean the building may collapse. Check for gas, water, and electrical damage. Do not use open flames indoors. If an appliance is wet and still plugged in, turn off power at the main breaker if you can do so safely and have an electrician check it before using it.
Source: CDC - After a hurricane <https://www.cdc.gov/hurricanes/safety/how-to-safely-stay-safe-after-a-hurricane-or-other-tropical-storm.html>; NWS - Hurricane Safety <https://www.weather.gov/media/safety/hurricane_safety.pdf>

## Water and food after the storm
Do not drink tap water until officials say it is safe. Until then, do not use it for drinking, making ice or baby formula, brushing teeth, washing food or dishes, or washing hands; use bottled, boiled, or treated water. Throw away perishable food that lost refrigeration, and any food with an odd smell, color, or texture.
Source: CDC - After a hurricane <https://www.cdc.gov/hurricanes/safety/how-to-safely-stay-safe-after-a-hurricane-or-other-tropical-storm.html>; NWS - Hurricane Safety <https://www.weather.gov/media/safety/hurricane_safety.pdf>

## Mosquitoes, animals and pests
Standing water after a storm breeds mosquitoes. Use insect repellent with DEET or picaridin and wear long sleeves, long pants, and socks outside. Stay away from wild or stray animals, which may be scared or injured; report them to animal control or the health department. Report dead animals to local officials.
Source: CDC - After a hurricane <https://www.cdc.gov/hurricanes/safety/how-to-safely-stay-safe-after-a-hurricane-or-other-tropical-storm.html>

## Generators after the storm
Many people are poisoned by carbon monoxide after hurricanes. Never use a generator, camp stove, or gas or charcoal equipment inside your home, basement, or garage. Keep it outside, at least 20 feet from windows, doors, and vents, and use a battery-powered CO alarm. If a CO alarm beeps, leave right away and call 911.
Source: CDC - After a hurricane <https://www.cdc.gov/hurricanes/safety/how-to-safely-stay-safe-after-a-hurricane-or-other-tropical-storm.html>
