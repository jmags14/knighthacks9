## Never leave pets behind
If you evacuate, take your pets with you. Pets left behind are unlikely to survive on their own. Before a storm, find out whether a pet-friendly shelter will open near you, and list hotels, boarding places, and vets that take animals, with their 24-hour phone numbers. Ask a neighbor or friend to check on or pick up your pet if you cannot get home.
Source: DLA summarizing Ready.gov <https://www.dla.mil/About-DLA/News/News-Article-View/Article/2788357/national-preparedness-month-includes-emergency-prepping-for-our-pets/>

## Pet emergency kit
Pack a small kit for each pet: food and water for at least 3 days, food and water bowls, medicines, a leash or carrier, a litter pan for cats, and a copy of tags and vaccination records. A favorite toy helps keep a pet calm. Remember to count your pet when you calculate how much water to store.
Source: DLA summarizing Ready.gov <https://www.dla.mil/About-DLA/News/News-Article-View/Article/2788357/national-preparedness-month-includes-emergency-prepping-for-our-pets/>

## Sheltering at home with pets
Choose a safe interior room where your family and pets can stay together, and put the pet's crate and supplies there before the storm arrives. During a tornado warning, bring pets with you to the safe room if there is time. Put a "pet inside" sticker on a front window or door so rescuers know an animal is in the home.
Source: DLA summarizing Ready.gov <https://www.dla.mil/About-DLA/News/News-Article-View/Article/2788357/national-preparedness-month-includes-emergency-prepping-for-our-pets/>; NWS - Tornado safety <https://www.weather.gov/safety/tornado-during>

## Identification
Get your pet microchipped and keep its contact information up to date. A microchip greatly improves the chance of finding a pet that gets lost during a storm. Keep a collar with ID tags on the pet.
