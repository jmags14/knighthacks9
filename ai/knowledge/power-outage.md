## First steps when the power goes out
Use flashlights or battery lanterns instead of candles, which can start fires. Unplug appliances and electronics, or use surge protectors, so a power surge does not damage them when power comes back. Keep the refrigerator and freezer closed. Follow any boil-water advisory, and use bottled or treated water if your water may be unsafe. Check on family and neighbors if it is safe to do so.
Source: Ready.gov (FEMA) - Power Outages <https://www.ready.gov/power-outages>

## Fumes and fire during an outage
Run generators, camp stoves, and charcoal grills only outdoors, at least 20 feet from windows, doors, and garages. Never use a gas stove, oven, grill, or dryer to heat or cool your home. Never run a vehicle inside a garage. Use fireplaces, portable heaters, and wood stoves carefully.
Source: Ready.gov (FEMA) - Power Outages <https://www.ready.gov/power-outages>

## Medical devices and medicines during an outage
If someone depends on a powered medical device, such as oxygen or a breathing machine, plan ahead with their doctor for how to power it. If the outage lasts long or the device cannot run, go somewhere with power if it is safe. Refrigerated medicines kept at room temperature should be replaced if the power was out for a day or more, unless the label says otherwise; ask a doctor or pharmacist, and keep using the medicine until you can get a new supply.
Source: Ready.gov (FEMA) - Power Outages <https://www.ready.gov/power-outages>

## Making the outage easier
Keep phones charged and have a backup battery. You can charge a phone in a car parked outside in an open area. Landlines may not work if lines are damaged. Keep your car's gas tank full before predicted storms. Know where you could go for air conditioning if the house gets too hot.
Source: Ready.gov (FEMA) - Power Outages <https://www.ready.gov/power-outages>

## After the power comes back
Throw away refrigerated food that was at 40 degrees F or warmer for more than 4 hours, or that has an unusual smell, color, or texture. When in doubt, throw it out. Plug appliances back in slowly. Check your home for damage before going outside.
Source: Ready.gov (FEMA) - Power Outages <https://www.ready.gov/power-outages>
