## Keeping insulin when the fridge is off
According to the FDA, insulin vials and pens (opened or unopened) can be kept unrefrigerated between 59 and 86 degrees F for up to 28 days. Keep insulin as cool as you can, out of direct sunlight and away from heat. If you use ice, do not let the insulin touch the ice directly. Never use insulin that has frozen. Insulin in a pump that was exposed to temperatures above 98.6 degrees F should be thrown away.
Source: FDA - Insulin storage in an emergency <https://www.fda.gov/drugs/emergency-preparedness-drugs/information-regarding-insulin-storage-and-switching-between-products-emergency>

## Insulin exposed to heat
Above 86 degrees F, insulin slowly loses strength the longer it stays hot. In an emergency, the FDA says you may still need to use insulin that got warmer than that. Replace any insulin that was exposed to extreme heat or cold as soon as properly stored insulin is available. Some newer insulins have different storage rules, so check the label.
Source: FDA - Insulin storage in an emergency <https://www.fda.gov/drugs/emergency-preparedness-drugs/information-regarding-insulin-storage-and-switching-between-products-emergency>

## Other refrigerated medicines
If refrigerated medicines sat at room temperature because the power was out for a day or more, plan to replace them unless the label says otherwise. Call a doctor or pharmacist when you can. Keep using the medicine until you can get a new supply, rather than going without.
Source: Ready.gov (FEMA) - Power Outages <https://www.ready.gov/power-outages>

## Powered medical equipment
If someone in the household uses oxygen, a breathing machine, or other powered medical equipment, talk to their doctor before storm season about how to keep it running without power. If the device cannot run, go somewhere with power if it is safe. In Florida, people who will need help or power during a disaster can register in advance with the Special Needs Registry (FloridaDisaster.org/SNR).
